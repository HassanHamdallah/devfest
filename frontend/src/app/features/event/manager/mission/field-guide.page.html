SENTINEL · Field Guide

You may use paper and a plain calculator.

Noor's notes on the tools you need to repair SENTINEL. Read them, then trust your own working.

Z-score

How many standard deviations a value sits from the mean:

Flag a point when |z| is above the threshold (2 or 3 are common).

For points with two coordinates, judge each axis on its own; a point is flagged if it is outside on either. The sd here is the population sd (divide by n, not n − 1).

Big outliers inflate the mean and sd, so they can hide each other.

IQR fences

Outside a fence means outlier. k = 1.5 is usual, k = 3 only catches extreme points. Medians and quartiles ignore outliers.

Quartiles here are Tukey's hinges: Q1 and Q3 are the medians of the lower and the upper half of the sorted values (with an odd count the middle value is in both halves). Each axis has its own fences; flagged if outside on either.

Leakage

A feature leaks when it would not be known at the moment of the prediction, or it gives the target away (a timestamp recorded after the event). Identifiers such as a plate number only memorise. Use what is known beforehand; drop the rest.

Neuron and ReLU

A neuron multiplies each input by its weight, adds the results and a bias:

An activation function then shapes the output. ReLU: a positive z passes unchanged, a negative z becomes 0.

KNN

Measure the distance to every labelled point, take the k nearest, and let them vote: the majority vote of those k labels is the answer. k = 1 trusts a single neighbour, so one odd point decides alone. A larger k smooths the noise; too large blurs the groups. An odd k avoids ties.

Precision: when it says yes, how often is it right. Recall: how many real ones it catches. Choose recall when missing one is the costly mistake.

Genetic algorithm

Routes (or any candidates) are written as bits. Two parents make a child:

  1. Crossover: pick a cut position; the child takes the start from one parent and the rest from the other.
  2. Mutation: flip one bit (0 becomes 1, 1 becomes 0).

A* search: f = g + h

g = cost so far, h = estimate of the cost still to go (never more than the real cost). Always expand the frontier place with the lowest f:

If two tie, follow the tie rule on the screen. Stop when the goal itself is expanded. Greedy search that looks at h alone can be fooled by a cheap-looking road.

Entropy and information gain

Entropy measures how mixed a group is: 0 when it is pure, 1 when two classes are equally common. The weighted average weighs each child by its share of the items. A bigger gain means a better split.

Minimax

Two players take turns: MAX wants the largest value, MIN wants the smallest. Work from the bottom of the tree: every MIN node keeps its smallest child value, every MAX node its largest. The value at the top is what MAX can guarantee.

RAG

Retrieval-augmented generation: split documents into chunks, retrieve the top-k chunks most similar to the question, and let the model answer from them. Chunk size: tiny chunks lose context, huge ones bring noise. Top-k: a bigger k adds evidence and distraction. Check what was retrieved before trusting the answer.

Submit each stage once: it locks. Experiments are limited in some stages; repeats are free.
