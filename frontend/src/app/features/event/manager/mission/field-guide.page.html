SENTINEL · Field Guide

Rami's notes on the tools you need to repair SENTINEL. Read them, then trust your own working.

Z-score

How many standard deviations a value sits from the mean: z = (x − mean) ÷ sd. Flag a point when |z| is above the threshold (2 or 3 are common).

For points with two coordinates, judge each axis on its own; a point is flagged if it is outside on either. The sd here is the population sd (divide by n, not n − 1).

Big outliers inflate the mean and sd, so they can hide each other.

IQR fences

IQR = Q3 − Q1. Fences: Q1 − k·IQR and Q3 + k·IQR. Outside a fence means outlier. k = 1.5 is usual, k = 3 only catches extreme points. Medians and quartiles ignore outliers.

Quartiles here are Tukey's hinges: Q1 and Q3 are the medians of the lower and the upper half of the sorted values (with an odd count the middle value is in both halves). Each axis has its own fences; flagged if outside on either.

Leakage

A feature leaks when it would not be known at the moment of the prediction, or it gives the target away (a timestamp recorded after the event). Identifiers such as a plate number only memorise. Use what is known beforehand; drop the rest.

Overfitting

The model learns the training data, noise included: training accuracy is high, validation accuracy (data it never saw) is much lower. Judge a model by validation. A simpler setting often wins.

KNN

Measure the distance to every labelled point, take the k nearest, and let them vote. k = 1 trusts a single neighbour, so one odd point decides alone. A larger k smooths the noise; too large blurs the groups. An odd k avoids ties.

Confusion metrics

said yessaid no
was yesTPFN
was noFPTN

precision = TP ÷ (TP + FP) (when it says yes, how often is it right). recall = TP ÷ (TP + FN) (how many real ones it catches). Choose recall when missing one is the costly mistake.

K-Means

  1. Start with k centroids.
  2. Assign every point to its nearest centroid.
  3. Update each centroid to the mean (x and y) of its points.
  4. Repeat 2 and 3 until nothing moves.

A* search: f = g + h

g = cost so far, h = estimate of the cost still to go (never more than the real cost). Always expand the frontier place with the lowest f = g + h; if two tie, follow the tie rule on the screen. Stop when the goal itself is expanded. Greedy search that looks at h alone can be fooled by a cheap-looking road.

Naive Bayes

For each class: prior × P(word₁ | class) × P(word₂ | class) × … over the words of the message. The larger value wins. "Naive" because it treats words as independent. A small prior can outweigh scary words.

Forward chaining

Start with known facts. A rule IF a AND b THEN c may fire only when all its conditions are known; then c becomes known. Keep firing rules until nothing new appears. The final conclusion is whatever the facts and rules prove, nothing more.

RAG

Retrieval-augmented generation: split documents into chunks, retrieve the top-k chunks most similar to the question, and let the model answer from them. Tiny chunks lose context; huge ones bring noise. A bigger k adds evidence and distraction. Check what was retrieved before trusting the answer.

Submit each stage once: it locks. Experiments are limited in some stages; repeats are free.
